Thank you for the review. All six items are answered below, and the same information is now in the Notes field of App Review Information. A screen recording captured on a physical iPhone is attached to this reply and to the version.

1. SCREEN RECORDING. Attached, recorded on a physical iPhone. It begins at app launch and shows onboarding, all five tabs, confirming a milestone, a source citation, writing a journal entry, Settings and the English/Korean switch, then Sign in with Apple (account registration), sign out, sign in again (login), and Settings > Account > Delete account (account deletion). There is no paid content or in-app purchase to show. Journal entries are private to the user's own account and are never visible to any other user, so there is no content reporting or blocking mechanism to demonstrate.

2. PURPOSE AND TARGET AUDIENCE. Sprout is for parents and caregivers of children aged 0-36 months; it is for adults and is not in the Kids category. Guidance on what a baby does at a given age is scattered across many websites and rarely tied to the child's exact age. Sprout puts the milestones, age-matched play ideas and home-safety notes for the child's exact month on one screen, each citing its public-health source, and keeps the parent's own record of what their child has done.

3. SETUP AND ACCESS. No login, no demo account and no sample files are required; every feature works without signing in. On first launch, type any name and a birthday (for example Rowoon, 2025-04-17) and tap Get started. The optional due-date field applies only to babies born 3+ weeks early. Five tabs: Home, Milestones, Play, Safety, Journal. Tap a leaf to confirm a milestone; tap the source name under any item for its citation. The gear icon on Home opens Settings. An optional account adds only cloud backup and sync of the user's own records; sign-in is by Sign in with Apple, Google, or an emailed link, so you can sign in with your own Apple ID and we have no credentials to supply. Sign out and Delete account are in Settings > Account; deletion asks for confirmation, then immediately removes the account and all data stored under it.

4. EXTERNAL SERVICES. Supabase (United States, AWS us-east-1) for authentication and the database holding a signed-in user's own records, contacted only if the user signs in. Apple (Sign in with Apple) and Google (OAuth) as identity providers, used only if chosen. Vercel hosts baby.minjae.co, serving the privacy policy, terms and support pages plus the one endpoint used by Delete account. No analytics, tracking, advertising, crash reporting or AI services, and no payment processors. Reminders are local notifications scheduled on the device; there is no remote push.

5. REGIONAL DIFFERENCES. None. The app behaves identically in every region and ships the same content everywhere. The only difference is the display language, English or Korean, picked by the user in Settings; on first launch it follows the device language, never the country or storefront.

6. REGULATED INDUSTRY AND THIRD-PARTY MATERIAL. Sprout is not a medical device and gives no diagnosis, treatment or personalised medical advice; a disclaimer appears during onboarding, on every source card, in Settings > About and in the Terms. No protected third-party material is reproduced: every milestone, play idea and safety note is our own short summary of public guidance from the CDC, AAP (HealthyChildren.org), WHO, Zero to Three, NAEYC, CPSC and Pathways.org, shown with the organisation's name, a link, and the date we last checked it. An automated audit of all 461 items confirmed none reproduces source text verbatim.
